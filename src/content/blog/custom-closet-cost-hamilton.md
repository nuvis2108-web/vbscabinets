It's one of the first questions we hear: how much does a custom closet cost? The honest answer is that it depends on the size of the closet, how you want it laid out, the materials and hardware you choose, and what the installation involves.

That said, nobody can plan a project around "it depends." So below you'll find the broad planning ranges we use when talking with homeowners in Hamilton, Burlington, Waterdown, Oakville, Milton and across the GTA, along with what pushes a closet toward the lower or upper end.

## Planning price ranges

| Closet type | Planning range |
| --- | --- |
| Basic reach-in closet | About $1,000–$2,500 |
| Larger or more customized reach-in closet | About $2,000–$4,000+ |
| Walk-in closet | About $3,500–$8,000+ |

**These are planning ranges only, not quotes or fixed prices.** Your actual price depends on the size of the space, materials, hardware, finish, site conditions and installation. The "+" matters: a large walk-in with lots of drawers, specialty hardware or a premium finish can go beyond the top of the range.

Use these numbers to decide whether a custom closet fits your budget and to start a conversation. Then get a quote based on your actual space.

## Why there's no single price for a custom closet

A custom closet is built to fit one specific space. Two closets that look similar in photos can have very different prices because of what's inside them and what the room is like. The main things that affect the cost are:

### Size and type of closet

A reach-in closet is a single wall of storage behind doors. A walk-in has storage on two or three walls and sometimes a centre island. More wall length usually means more material, more components and more installation time.

### Layout and interior components

Hanging rods and open shelves are the simplest parts of a closet. Drawers, pull-out baskets, shoe shelving, tie and belt organizers, and islands add both material and hardware. A layout with a lot of drawers will cost more than one built mostly around shelving and hanging space, even in the same size closet.

### Materials and finish

The material and finish you choose for the boxes, shelves and fronts make a real difference to the price. Painted or specialty finishes usually cost more than standard ones. If you have a look in mind, send us an inspiration photo and we'll talk through options at different price points.

### Doors

Many reach-in closets are left open or keep their existing doors. Others get new hinged doors, drawer fronts or sliding closet doors. New doors add to the cost, but they can change the look of the whole room.

### Hardware

Drawer slides, hinges, rods, handles and any integrated lighting all add up. Soft-close hardware and specialty accessories are worth it for many people, but they're one of the places a budget can stretch quickly.

### Installation and site conditions

Walls that aren't square, sloped ceilings, baseboards, outlets in awkward spots, or old shelving that needs to come out can all add time. We plan around these, which is why photos of your existing space help so much.

## Reach-in vs walk-in: where the budget usually goes

In a **reach-in closet**, the budget is mostly about using a small space well: double hanging where it makes sense, adjustable shelves, and maybe a short bank of drawers. A basic reach-in tends to sit in the lower range. Adding drawers, accessories or new doors moves it toward the "larger or more customized" range.

In a **walk-in closet**, there's simply more of everything: more wall length, more components and more installation time. Drawers, an island, shoe walls and specialty finishes are what usually push a walk-in toward the upper end of its range.

## How to keep the cost in check

You don't need every feature to get a closet that works well. A few ways to manage the budget:

- **Put drawers where they count.** A single bank of drawers is often enough. Shelving and hanging space cost less per foot.
- **Mix open and closed storage.** Open shelving for things you reach for daily and drawers for smaller items keeps the layout practical without overbuilding.
- **Keep the finish simple and spend on hardware.** Good slides and hinges make a closet feel solid every day.
- **Plan for the clothes you actually own.** Measuring your long-hanging items and counting shoes helps us size each section properly, so nothing is wasted.
