If you're planning a kitchen, laundry room or storage wall, you'll quickly run into the choice between custom cabinets and premade cabinets. Neither one is better in every case. The right choice depends on your space, your budget, and how much an exact fit and finish matter to you.

Here's a plain comparison to help you decide, from a cabinetmaker that builds custom work for homes in Hamilton, Burlington, Waterdown, Oakville, Milton and the GTA.

## What "premade" cabinets are

Premade cabinets are built ahead of time in standard sizes, usually in fixed width steps. They come in a few forms:

- **Stock cabinets:** ready-made boxes in set sizes, colours and door styles.
- **Ready-to-assemble (RTA) cabinets:** flat-packed and assembled on site.
- **Semi-custom cabinets:** standard boxes with more choice of sizes, finishes and accessories.

Because the sizes are fixed, a premade layout fills a wall with the closest standard widths and covers the remaining gaps with filler strips.

## What custom cabinets are

Custom cabinets are designed and built for your specific room. Every box is sized to your measurements, and the layout, door style, finish and hardware are chosen for your project. Custom cabinets can follow a sloped ceiling, run full height, wrap around a window, or combine two finishes in one room.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Premade cabinets | Custom cabinets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit | Standard sizes, gaps closed with fillers | Built to your measurements |
| Layout | Limited to available sizes and configurations | Designed around your room and how you use it |
| Finish and style | Choose from a set range | Chosen for your project, including two-tone and matched finishes |
| Odd spaces | Hard to use well (slopes, bulkheads, corners) | Can be planned in from the start |
| Lead time | Often shorter if items are in stock | Built to order, so planning starts earlier |
| Cost | Usually lower up front | Usually higher, depending on scope |
| Installation | Standard boxes, adjusted on site | Planned and installed as one built piece |

Quality varies on both sides. There are good and poor premade cabinets, and custom cabinets are only as good as the materials, hardware and workmanship that go into them. Ask any supplier what the boxes, doors and hardware are made of.

## When premade cabinets make sense

Premade cabinets can be a good choice when:

- The room is a standard shape, and standard sizes fill it without much waste
- Budget is the main priority and the layout is simple
- You need cabinets quickly and the style you want is in stock
- It's a secondary space, like a basic utility room, where a perfect fit matters less

We'd rather you choose the right option for your home than oversell custom work you don't need.

## When custom cabinets are worth it

Custom cabinetry usually earns its cost when:

- **The room isn't standard.** Sloped ceilings, bulkheads, uneven walls and tight corners are where premade sizes leave wasted space.
- **You want storage to the ceiling.** Full-height cabinets and pantry walls make better use of a room.
- **Finish matters.** Two-tone kitchens, matching islands, and cabinets that coordinate with built-ins elsewhere in the house are easier to get right when everything is built together.
- **It's a long-term home.** If you plan to stay, a layout built around how you cook and store things pays off every day.

## A middle path: custom where it counts

You don't have to choose one approach for the whole house. Some homeowners put custom cabinetry in the kitchen, where fit and finish matter most, and use simpler storage in a basement or utility room. Others go custom only on one awkward wall. We're happy to talk through where custom work makes the biggest difference in your space.

## What affects the price of custom cabinets

We don't publish one price range for custom cabinetry, because "a kitchen" can mean anything from a short run of cabinets to a full room with an island and pantry wall. Pricing varies a lot depending on:

- **Cabinet count and size:** the number of boxes, and whether they run full height
- **Material:** what the boxes, shelves and doors are made from
- **Door style:** simple slab doors vs shaker or more detailed profiles
- **Finish:** painted, stained, two-tone or specialty finishes
- **Hardware:** hinges, drawer slides, pull-outs, organizers and handles
- **Countertops:** material and size, if they're part of the scope
- **Installation:** site conditions, removal of existing cabinets, and any adjustments the room needs

**Any price you see before a quote should be treated as a rough planning number only.** Actual pricing depends on size, materials, hardware, finish, site conditions and installation. The quickest way to a real number is a quote based on your space and the scope you want.
