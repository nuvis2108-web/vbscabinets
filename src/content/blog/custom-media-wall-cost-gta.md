A custom media wall can turn a plain living room wall into the centrepiece of the room, with a place for the TV, storage for everything around it, and shelving or lighting that makes it feel built in. Naturally, the next question is what it costs.

Every media wall is designed for one specific wall, so there's no fixed price. But there are broad planning ranges, and some clear factors decide where your project lands. Here's how we explain it to homeowners in Hamilton, Burlington, Waterdown, Oakville, Milton and across the GTA.

## Planning price ranges

| Media wall type | Planning range |
| --- | --- |
| Media wall | About $3,000–$7,000+ |
| More elaborate media wall with a fireplace, more cabinetry, lighting or specialty wall panels | About $5,000–$10,000+ |

**These are planning ranges only, not quotes or fixed prices.** Your actual price depends on the size of the wall, materials, hardware, finish, site conditions and installation. Larger or more detailed designs can go beyond the top of these ranges.

Use them to see whether the project fits your budget and which features matter most to you. Then get a quote for your actual wall.

## What counts as a media wall

"Media wall" covers a lot of designs. A media wall might include any of these:

- A **TV wall** with a feature panel behind the screen
- **Fluted or slatted panels** for texture
- **Open shelving** or arched niches on either side
- **Base cabinets** for closed storage along the bottom
- A **fireplace surround** with built-ins around it
- **Integrated lighting** in the shelves or niches

A simple design might be a feature panel and a run of base cabinets. A larger one might use the full width and height of the wall, with shelving, cabinets, lighting and a fireplace. That difference in scope is the main reason the ranges are wide.

## What affects the cost

### Size of the wall

Width and height drive the amount of material and the time to build and install. A wall-to-wall, floor-to-ceiling design costs more than a unit that fills the centre of the wall.

### Design elements

Each element adds material and work. Fluted or slatted panels, arched openings, open shelving and closed cabinets each add to the cost in their own way. A design that combines several of them will sit higher in the range than one built around a single feature.

### Lighting

LED lighting in shelves or niches adds parts and wiring work, and new outlets or circuits may need an electrician. If you want lighting, it's best to plan it from the start so wiring can be hidden inside the build.

### Fireplace integration

Building around a fireplace means planning for the fireplace manufacturer's clearance requirements around the opening, the mantel and nearby materials. A fireplace wall usually involves more detailed work, which is why it's in the higher range.

### Finish and materials

Paint colour, wood shelving, stained details and hardware all affect the price. A two-material design, such as painted cabinets with wood shelves, takes more work than a single finish.

### TV, sound and cables

Planning for your TV size, a soundbar, media equipment and hidden cable routes is part of the design. Telling us your TV size and what equipment you have up front avoids changes later.

### Site conditions and installation

Wall condition, existing outlets, baseboards, flooring transitions and access to the room all affect installation time.

## Planning your budget

If you have a set budget, decide which features matter most to you:

- **Storage first:** base cabinets give the most practical value per foot.
- **One feature, done well:** a single fluted panel or a pair of arched niches can carry the design without adding every element.
- **Plan lighting early:** adding it later is harder than building it in.
- **Size to the room:** a media wall doesn't have to fill the full width to look built in.
